Exercise: ANOVA & Correlation

What you will do

  1. Pick one of the four datasets below.
  2. Run a one-way ANOVA (a numeric outcome across a categorical group). If the result is significant, run a Tukey test to see which groups differ.
  3. Run a correlation analysis on two numeric variables.

How to load a dataset

library(tidyverse)
library(mosaic)

read.csv("url of the data") %>%
  as_tibble() -> data

Pick one dataset

1. World Happiness

The World Happiness Report scores countries on how happy people say they are, and estimates how much different factors contribute.

Variable Type Meaning
country non numeric country name
region categorical Europe, Americas, Asia, Africa & Middle East
happiness_score numeric average happiness ladder score (0–10)
gdp_per_capita numeric the report’s GDP-per-capita score (higher = richer)
social_support numeric having someone to count on
life_expectancy numeric healthy-life-expectancy score
freedom numeric freedom to make life choices
generosity numeric generosity score
country region happiness_score gdp_per_capita social_support life_expectancy freedom generosity
Switzerland Europe 7.587 1.3965 1.3495 0.9414 0.6656 0.2968
Iceland Europe 7.561 1.3023 1.4022 0.9478 0.6288 0.4363
Denmark Europe 7.527 1.3255 1.3606 0.8746 0.6494 0.3414
Norway Europe 7.522 1.4590 1.3310 0.8852 0.6697 0.3470
Canada Americas 7.427 1.3263 1.3226 0.9056 0.6330 0.4581
  • ANOVA: does average happiness_score differ across regions?
  • Correlation: is gdp_per_capita correlated with happiness_score? (Does money buy happiness?)
  • Also try: is generosity correlated with happiness_score?

2. FIFA Football Players

Ratings and market values of footballers from the FIFA 19 database. Think of a player’s market value as the price of talent in a labour market.

Variable Type Meaning
name, nationality, club non numeric who the player is
position categorical Goalkeeper, Defender, Midfielder, Forward
reputation categorical international fame: 1-star (least) … 3-star (most)
age numeric age in years
age_group categorical age band: 21 or under, 22–25, 26–29, 30+
overall numeric overall skill rating (0–100)
potential numeric potential rating
value_million_eur numeric market value, in million €
wage_thousand_eur numeric weekly wage, in thousand €
name age age_group nationality club position overall potential value_million_eur wage_thousand_eur reputation
Coutinho 26 26-29 Brazil FC Barcelona Forward 88 89 69.5 340 3-star
S. Milinković-Savić 23 22-25 Serbia Lazio Midfielder 85 90 50.5 73 2-star

T. Lemar 22 22-25 France Atlético Madrid Midfielder 83 89 35.5 64 3-star
Allan 27 26-29 Brazil Napoli Midfielder 83 83 26.5 87 2-star
  • ANOVA: does value_million_eur differ across age_groups? (Do players peak in market value at a certain age?)
  • Correlation: is overall rating correlated with value_million_eur? (Does skill set the price?)
  • Also try: does value_million_eur differ across positions? (Are forwards worth more than defenders — or not?)

3. Box Office

Budgets, ratings and worldwide box-office takings for Hollywood films. Think of a film as an investment.

Variable Type Meaning

genre categorical Action, Adventure, Comedy, Drama, Horror
budget_million numeric production budget, in million US$
worldwide_gross_million numeric worldwide box-office, in million US$
imdb_rating numeric IMDb rating (0–10)
rotten_tomatoes numeric Rotten Tomatoes score (0–100)
running_time_min numeric length in minutes
mpaa_rating non numeric MPAA rating (G, PG, PG-13, R, …)
title genre budget_million worldwide_gross_million imdb_rating rotten_tomatoes running_time_min mpaa_rating

  • Also try: is imdb_rating correlated with worldwide_gross_million? (Do better-rated films earn more?)

Recent US graduates by major: what they earn, how many are women, and how often they’re unemployed (US Census ACS data).

median_earnings numeric median earnings of recent graduates (US$)
share_women numeric share of graduates who are women (0–1)
unemployment_rate numeric unemployment rate (0–1)
grads_total numeric number of graduates
